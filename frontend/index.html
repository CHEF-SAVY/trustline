Borrow on trust, not just collateral.

TrustLine reads a borrower's XRPL history inside a secure enclave, then signs only the verdict. The evidence never leaves.

The problem

Every DeFi loan asks you to over-deposit.

Put up $1,000 of collateral, borrow $500. Not because you have money spare — but because the contract has no idea who you are.

Today

Overcollateralization stands in for trust.

A pool that can't assess you simply demands enough collateral that it doesn't need to care whether you disappear.

The catch

Your history could prove you're good for it — at a price.

XRPL is public, so the evidence exists. But using it on-chain means publishing your balances, counterparties and volumes permanently, to everyone. Most people won't. So everyone stays at 50%.

How it works

The assessment happens inside a sealed box.

Flare's Confidential Compute runs code in hardware enclaves that the machine's own operator cannot see into. We put the credit check in there.

  1. 01

    You request a check

    One transaction naming the XRPL address you want assessed.

  2. 02

    The enclave reads your history

    Account age, transaction count, payment volume, counterparty diversity — all computed inside, none of it emitted.

  3. 03

    It signs a verdict

    Six values: address, a hash of your XRPL account, a tier, a maximum LTV, and two timestamps. Nothing else.

  4. 04

    The pool verifies and lends

    The contract confirms the signature came from a registered enclave, then lends against the tier.

Standard, overcollateralized 50%
Underwritten by TrustLine 75%

Same $1,000 collateral. $750 instead of $500 — and the history behind that number was never published.

What actually stays private

Six values on-chain. Nothing behind them.

We'd rather be precise about this than impressive. Here is the whole disclosure, and the one thing it doesn't cover.

Published

  • Your wallet address
  • A hash of your XRPL address
  • Risk tier, 0–3
  • Maximum LTV
  • Issued-at and expiry

Never leaves the enclave

  • Transaction history
  • Counterparties
  • Balances and volumes
  • Account age
  • Every intermediate score

Stated plainly

The XRPL address you ask about is visible in the request itself — the enclave has to be told what to score. So the link between your wallet and your XRPL account is public. What stays confidential is the evidence behind your score, not the fact that you asked for one. A determined attacker can also age accounts to game the tier; the model raises that cost, it doesn't eliminate it.
